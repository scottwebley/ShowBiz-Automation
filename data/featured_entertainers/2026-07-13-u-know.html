FEATURED ENTERTAINER OF THE WEEK

U-KNOW

Singer

July 13, 2026

TVXQ!'s U-KNOW brings first solo concert tour 'PROJECT 26 : SCENE#1' to Singapore this August

For more than two decades, U-KNOW has been the kind of performer who treats the stage less like a platform than a pressure chamber. In TVXQ!, he helped define the high-polish, high-athleticism model that K-pop would later export around the world. As a solo artist, he has gone in a different direction: cinematic mini-albums, character-driven performances and a fascination with showmanship that feels closer to theatre than a standard pop rollout. That is why the announcement of his first solo concert tour matters. PROJECT 26 : SCENE#1, with a Singapore stop set for 15 August at The Theatre at Mediacorp, is not simply another itinerary on the Asian concert calendar. It is a late-career first from an artist who has already performed in some of the largest venues in Asia with TVXQ!, yet is now testing what happens when the full architecture of a show rests on him alone. U-KNOW, born Jung Yun-ho, has long been understood as TVXQ!'s leader, its kinetic centre and one of SM Entertainment's defining stage presences. But his solo work has gradually revealed another ambition: to build a performance universe where dance, narrative, lighting, costume and character operate as one. This week's tour news arrives at a point when many second-generation K-pop stars are being re-evaluated not as nostalgia acts, but as working artists with deep catalogues, loyal audiences and the discipline to keep evolving. U-KNOW belongs at the centre of that conversation.

Early Life

Long before he became U-KNOW, Jung Yun-ho was a teenager from Gwangju with an unusually serious idea of what performance could be. His stage name is commonly read as a play on 'Yunho' and the phrase 'you know', a fitting choice for someone whose public identity has always depended on projection, confidence and direct contact with an audience. He grew up far from Seoul's entertainment centre, and his route into the industry was not frictionless. Yunho has spoken over the years about difficult trainee days, including financial strain and the uncertainty of trying to break into an industry that produces far more hopefuls than stars. What separated him was not only dance ability, though that was central, but stamina. SM Entertainment's trainee system in the early 2000s demanded long hours, rapid correction and a willingness to be shaped into a group performer. Yunho entered that system after being recognized through SM's audition process and became known as a strong dancer before debut. He trained during a period when K-pop was still consolidating its modern idol format: synchronized choreography, fan culture, Korean television promotion, Japanese-market ambition and carefully managed group identity. Those conditions would become the foundation of TVXQ!, and Yunho arrived at exactly the moment when the industry was ready for a group with scale.

Career

U-KNOW debuted in December 2003 as the leader of TVXQ!, the SM Entertainment group originally launched as a five-member act with U-KNOW Yunho, Max Changmin, Hero Jaejoong, Micky Yoochun and Xiah Junsu. The timing was crucial. Korean pop had already produced major idol acts, but TVXQ! arrived with a particular mix of vocal ambition, elaborate performance and dramatic visual presentation. Their debut single 'Hug' introduced them as youthful and polished; later records such as 'Rising Sun', 'O-Jung.Ban.Hap.' and 'Mirotic' pushed them into a more intense register. Yunho's role was not just administrative leadership. He became the physical anchor of the group: the member whose dance lines, force and sharp timing gave TVXQ! much of its stage identity. The group quickly became one of the most important Korean acts of the 2000s, building enormous followings in South Korea and Japan. In Japan, where they promoted as Tohoshinki, TVXQ! became a landmark case in how a Korean act could build a durable career rather than chase a brief crossover moment. They learned the language, worked the television circuit, released Japanese material and toured relentlessly. The achievement was not glamorous in the abstract; it was built on repetition, scheduling and the willingness to start again in a market where they were not automatically stars. That discipline paid off. TVXQ!'s Japanese tours eventually reached stadium and dome scale, including historic performances at Nissan Stadium, and helped open doors for later generations of Korean artists. The career also carried a dramatic rupture. In 2009, three TVXQ! members entered a legal dispute with SM Entertainment, and the original five-member formation effectively ended. For many groups, that kind of fracture can freeze the legacy in the past. U-KNOW and Max Changmin instead returned as a duo in 2011 with 'Keep Your Head Down', one of the most consequential comeback statements in second-generation K-pop. The title itself sounded like a challenge, and the performance was built around severity, precision and theatrical aggression. As a duo, TVXQ! had to solve an artistic problem in public: how to preserve the scale of a five-member act with only two performers. Yunho's command of stage space became even more important. He and Changmin reconfigured arrangements, choreography and vocal distribution, turning absence into a new identity rather than pretending nothing had changed. U-KNOW's solo career developed more slowly, which is part of what makes the current tour announcement so interesting. He did not rush to detach himself from TVXQ!'s brand. Instead, his solo releases have felt curated. His first Korean solo mini-album, 'True Colors', arrived in 2019, long after he had already secured veteran status. The project introduced him not as a newly independent idol, but as a performer interested in mood and concept. 'Noir' in 2021 went further, structuring its songs around cinematic genres and character studies. 'Reality Show' in 2023 continued that fascination with image, fiction and identity. U-KNOW's solo music is not simply a collection of songs; it often behaves like a storyboard. His acting career has followed a similarly exploratory path. He took on leading television roles in 'Heading to the Ground', 'I Order You' and 'Meloholic', and supporting or ensemble parts in dramas including 'Queen of Ambition', 'The Night Watchman's Journal' and 'Race'. Idol acting can be a harsh arena, and Yunho's screen work has been met with varying levels of critical response, but the willingness to keep trying matters to his larger story. His strengths have always been tied to physicality, timing and presence, and his best solo work uses those qualities rather than trying to disguise them. The same is true of his famous resilience. In 2006, he was hospitalized after an anti-fan handed him a drink laced with glue during a filming schedule, an incident that became one of the most disturbing examples of the dangers idol performers can face. He returned to work and remained a public figure defined less by victimhood than endurance. His military service from 2015 to 2017 also became part of his public image. Yunho served in the South Korean army and was recognized for exemplary service, including a special-class warrior distinction. In K-pop, enlistment can interrupt momentum, but for Yunho it reinforced the traits fans already associated with him: discipline, seriousness and an almost old-fashioned sense of duty. When he returned, he stepped back into TVXQ! activities and then opened the door to a more fully articulated solo identity. That is the context behind PROJECT 26 : SCENE#1. A first solo concert tour after more than 20 years in entertainment is not a beginner's milestone; it is a reintroduction. U-KNOW has already stood in front of vast crowds as part of TVXQ!, but a solo tour asks a different question. What does he want the audience to see when there is no group formation around him, no shared mythology to lean on, and no need to compress himself into the role of leader? The answer, if his recent solo projects are any guide, will likely be something meticulously staged, dance-forward and built around transformation.

Why ShowBiz Selected This Entertainer

U-KNOW is featured this week because the announcement of PROJECT 26 : SCENE#1 marks a rare kind of milestone: a first solo concert tour from an artist who is already a veteran of arena, dome and stadium-scale performance. The Singapore stop on 15 August at The Theatre at Mediacorp gives the news a regional focus, but the larger story is career-wide. U-KNOW helped build TVXQ! into one of K-pop's foundational global acts, survived a major group rupture, returned as part of a successful duo and then waited until his solo identity had real shape before taking it on the road. This tour is not a break from his legacy. It is a new frame for it.
