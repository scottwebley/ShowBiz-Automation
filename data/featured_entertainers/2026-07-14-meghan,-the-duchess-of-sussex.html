Meghan, the Duchess of Sussex

Television personality and former actress

July 14, 2026

Meghan earns first Daytime Emmy nomination for Netflix show

For years, Meghan, the Duchess of Sussex, has been discussed as a royal figure, a public advocate, a tabloid obsession, a podcast host, a producer, a brand founder and, sometimes almost as an afterthought, a television performer. This week moved the conversation back to the screen. Her Netflix lifestyle series, With Love, Meghan, earned a Daytime Emmy nomination in the outstanding lifestyle programme category, marking her first Daytime Emmy recognition and giving her post-royal media career a new kind of industry validation. The nomination matters because Meghan’s career has always been shaped by reinvention. Before she became one of the most recognisable women in the world, she was a working actress with a steady cable drama role, a side career in lifestyle writing and the unusual discipline of someone who had spent years hearing no before one yes changed everything. After Suits, marriage into the British royal family could have turned her screen career into a closed chapter. Instead, it became the prologue to a more complicated second act: documentaries, a Spotify podcast, a production company, a lifestyle brand and now an Emmy-nominated daytime series built around hosting, hospitality and domestic creativity. With Love, Meghan is not a return to acting. It is closer to a return to authorship. Long before the duchess title, Meghan was writing about food, travel, friendship and taste on her lifestyle site The Tig. The Netflix show revives that sensibility for a very different stage: not a personal blog followed by fans of a cable actress, but a streaming series made under a global deal, viewed through the glare of celebrity, royalty and public debate. The Daytime Emmy nomination suggests that, beneath the noise, television voters saw a programme that fits into a long tradition of lifestyle entertainment — practical, aspirational, personal and host-driven. That is why Meghan is ShowBiz.com’s Featured Entertainer this week. The nomination is the news hook, but the larger story is how a Los Angeles actor who learned television from the inside — call sheets, auditions, guest parts, ensemble drama and unscripted hosting — has built one of the most scrutinised media careers of the streaming era.

Early Life

Rachel Meghan Markle grew up in Los Angeles, close enough to the entertainment business to understand both its glamour and its machinery. Her father, Thomas Markle, worked as a lighting director and director of photography, including on network television productions, which meant Meghan saw studio life before she ever became a professional performer. Her mother, Doria Ragland, worked in social work and yoga instruction, and Meghan has often spoken of her mother’s steadiness and influence. She attended Immaculate Heart High School, an all-girls Catholic school in Los Angeles, before studying at Northwestern University, where she pursued theatre and international studies. That combination is a useful key to her later life. Meghan did not train only as an actor; she also developed an interest in diplomacy, social issues and public communication. During college, she interned at the U.S. Embassy in Buenos Aires, an experience that pointed toward the global-facing work she would later pursue through advocacy and media. Like many actors, her early professional years were uneven. She took auditions, worked small parts and used calligraphy skills for freelance jobs, including wedding invitations and celebrity correspondence. That detail has often been treated as charming trivia, but it says something more practical: before Suits made her recognisable, Meghan knew the freelance life. She understood how performers patch together income, visibility and opportunity while waiting for a role substantial enough to change the pattern.

Career

Meghan’s screen career began the way many Los Angeles acting careers begin: with small roles that required presence in a short amount of screen time. She appeared in episodes of shows including General Hospital, CSI: NY, 90210, Knight Rider, Without a Trace, Fringe and Castle. She also worked as a briefcase model on Deal or No Deal, a job she later described as formative but not creatively fulfilling. It placed her on a hit daytime-adjacent game show, but not yet in the kind of role she wanted. The turning point came in 2011 with Suits, the USA Network legal drama that became one of cable television’s durable hits. Meghan played Rachel Zane, a paralegal with ambitions of becoming an attorney. The role was significant because it allowed her to be more than a guest star moving through someone else’s plot. Rachel was intelligent, stylish, vulnerable and driven; she was also central to the show’s long-running romantic storyline with Mike Ross, played by Patrick J. Adams. Suits gave Meghan seven seasons of steady visibility and the discipline of ensemble television, where character development depends not on one spectacular scene but on years of small choices. The show’s success also gave her a platform beyond acting. In 2014, Meghan launched The Tig, a lifestyle website named after Tignanello wine. It mixed travel notes, recipes, interviews, fashion, personal essays and reflections on public service. At the time, celebrity lifestyle sites were becoming a genre of their own, but The Tig had a slightly different tone: more conversational, more cosmopolitan, and rooted in Meghan’s experience as a working actress who travelled, cooked, entertained and wrote with genuine enthusiasm. In retrospect, With Love, Meghan feels less like a sudden pivot and more like a continuation of interests she had been developing long before she entered royal life. Her relationship with Prince Harry changed the scale of everything. Meghan and Harry announced their engagement in 2017 and married at St George’s Chapel, Windsor Castle, in May 2018. She left Suits after its seventh season and closed The Tig before the wedding. As the Duchess of Sussex, she took on royal patronages and public work, with particular focus on women’s empowerment, education and community organisations. Her time as a working royal was brief and turbulent, shaped by intense press attention, race, class, monarchy, family conflict and the demands of royal institution life. In 2020, Meghan and Harry stepped back as senior working members of the royal family and moved to North America. That decision transformed them from royal representatives into independent public figures building a media, philanthropic and business portfolio. They founded Archewell, which includes charitable and production work, and signed major deals with Netflix and Spotify. Their 2022 Netflix documentary series Harry & Meghan drew enormous attention because it was both personal testimony and a media event, allowing the couple to frame their story in their own words. The path was not without setbacks. Archetypes, Meghan’s Spotify podcast, debuted strongly and won a Gracie Award, but the Spotify partnership ended after one season. Public reaction to the Sussexes’ projects has often been sharply divided, with their work judged not only as entertainment but as statements in an ongoing cultural argument about monarchy, celebrity, privacy and influence. For many entertainers, a show is simply a show. For Meghan, almost every project becomes evidence in a larger public debate. That is part of what makes the Daytime Emmy nomination for With Love, Meghan notable. It separates the work, however briefly, from the storm around it. Lifestyle television depends on trust: the host must make viewers believe there is pleasure in a table setting, a recipe, a garden visit, a conversation with a friend. Meghan is not trying to disappear into a character here; she is using her own taste and persona as the format. The nomination places her in a lineage of hosts and producers who turn domestic and social rituals into television. Her forthcoming appearance on MasterChef Australia extends that same trajectory. It places her not in a royal interview chair or documentary confessional, but in the popular international language of food television. For someone whose public image has so often been debated in political or royal terms, the move is commercially smart: cooking competitions and lifestyle shows meet audiences in a warmer register. They allow personality to emerge through enthusiasm, timing and conversation rather than through formal statements. Meghan’s career has always sat at the intersection of entertainment and identity. She is a former actress whose most famous role may still be herself, but that description undersells the craft involved in building a modern media presence. She knows television production from years on set. She understands lifestyle storytelling from The Tig. She understands global attention from royal life. The challenge now is whether she can turn that unusual combination into durable programming. This week’s Emmy nomination is a sign that, at least within the daytime television world, the answer is beginning to take shape.
